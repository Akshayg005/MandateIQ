-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH VERSION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

When a subscription payment fails, every system in the world asks the same
question: "will a retry succeed?" It then retries on a fixed calendar and
gives up after three tries. This project asks a different question - "which
of three things actually went wrong?" - and picks a different action for
each. Sometimes that action is to stop trying and offer the customer a clean
way out. It is the only recovery engine we know of whose recommended action
is sometimes "let them go," and it can prove with numbers that doing so
makes more money over time than grinding.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. THE PROBLEM, IN PLAIN WORDS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A customer subscribes to something and gives their bank standing permission
to debit them every month. That permission is called a MANDATE. Most months
the debit works. Some months it doesn't - the balance is short, the card
expired, the bank timed out.

When that debit fails, the merchant has not lost a customer who wanted to
leave. They have lost a customer who wanted to stay and whose bank said no.
That is called INVOLUNTARY CHURN, and across subscription businesses it
accounts for somewhere between 20% and 40% of all churn. It is the largest
silent leak in the business model.

What everyone does about it is retry on a fixed schedule. Razorpay's own
documented behaviour: when a subscription payment fails, it retries once a
day for three days, and if all three fail the subscription is halted.

That schedule is a guess. It does not know why the debit failed. It knows
nothing about that customer's money rhythm. And it treats a Rs 199 music
subscription exactly like a Rs 40,000 software mandate.

Worse, that single rule collapses two completely different people into one
outcome:

  Person A was Rs 300 short on the 2nd, 3rd and 4th of the month.
            -> The system just killed a paying customer who wanted to stay.

  Person B wants to cancel but can't be bothered to figure out how, so
            they are letting the debit bounce on purpose.
            -> The system just spent four attempts and three notifications
               harassing someone who was trying to leave.

Both get retried three times. Both get halted. Neither gets the right
treatment.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3. WHY NOW - THE THING THAT CHANGED FOUR MONTHS AGO
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

On 21 April 2026 the Reserve Bank of India issued the "Digital Payments -
E-mandate Framework, 2026" (circular RBI/DPSS/2026-27/396). It consolidated
every previous recurring-payment rule into one framework, and it changed the
economics of retrying.

The clauses that matter:

  Clause 6(a)  The issuer must send the customer a notification at least
               24 HOURS BEFORE every debit.

  Clause 6(c)  That notification must carry an opt-out - and the customer
               can opt out of THAT TRANSACTION, or of THE ENTIRE MANDATE.
               The opt-out is authenticated, so it is a real, final action.

  Clause 8(a)  Debits up to Rs 15,000 can go through without the customer
               authenticating. Above that, they must authenticate every time.

  Clause 8(b)  The limit rises to Rs 1,00,000 for insurance premiums,
               mutual fund subscriptions, and credit card bill payments.

  Clause 4(c)  On variable mandates, the customer sets a maximum amount per
               transaction. You may never attempt above it.

  Clause 10(c) Acquirers - payment aggregators like Razorpay - are
               responsible for ensuring the merchants they onboard comply.

Now follow the consequence, because it is the whole reason this project
exists:

  A RETRY IS A DEBIT. A DEBIT REQUIRES 24 HOURS' NOTICE. EVERY NOTICE
  IS A CANCEL BUTTON PLACED IN THE CUSTOMER'S HAND.

Retries used to be free. Worst case, nothing happened. Now every retry
attempt is also a reminder that this subscription exists, delivered with a
one-tap way to end it. A three-day retry ladder sends three cancellation
invitations in three days to a customer who is already annoyed.

There is a second consequence, and it is even sharper:

  YOU CANNOT RETRY REACTIVELY ANY MORE.

Because the issuer needs 24 hours to notify, the merchant must COMMIT to a
retry time a full day before it fires. Anything you learn in that 24-hour
window is unusable. You are forecasting, not reacting.

This breaks the approach used elsewhere in the world. Stripe's Smart Retries
picks retry timing by reacting to live signals - how many devices have
presented that card in the last few hours, and so on. That method is
structurally unavailable in India. The dominant global solution does not
transfer.

There is also a hard budget. NPCI permits ONE original attempt plus THREE
retries. Four attempts total, ever. This is not a tuning parameter.

And there is a legal edge. India's consumer protection authority published
Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns in 2023, which
name "subscription trap" - easy to subscribe, hard to cancel - as one of
thirteen prohibited practices. Enforcement is active in 2026, with penalties
issued and show-cause notices sent. Grinding a customer who is trying to
leave is no longer merely unkind; it is exposure. And under clause 10(c),
some of that exposure lands on the aggregator.

One honest note: the RBI circular never uses the word "retry." Whether a
reattempt needs its own fresh 24-hour notification, or is covered by the
original one, is genuinely unresolved. We do not pretend to know. We build
BOTH interpretations and report results under each. That ambiguity is real
and engineering for it is a deliberate design choice.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 4. WHAT WE BUILD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An engine that, for every failed recurring debit, infers which of three
things is actually true, and acts accordingly.

  STATE 1 - CAN'T PAY NOW
    Meaning: temporary liquidity gap. The money isn't there today.
    Action:  spend one of the four attempts, timed to when that customer's
             account is most likely to have money.

  STATE 2 - CAN'T PAY EVER
    Meaning: the payment instrument is dead. Card expired, account closed,
             mandate revoked.
    Action:  stop retrying immediately - it is futile and, for a revoked
             mandate, a compliance problem. Ask the customer to re-authorise.

  STATE 3 - WON'T PAY
    Meaning: they want out and are passively resisting.
    Action:  OFFER a clean exit. Pause first, then downgrade, then cancel.
             Never execute it - offer it.

The third state is the one nobody ships. Every dunning product on the market
optimises recovery rate, so "let them go" is not in their action space.

Why pause first: Razorpay's own published research says roughly 10% of
customers who cancel auto-renewal would have preferred to pause if that were
an option. Razorpay's APIs support pausing and resuming subscriptions and
UPI mandates. So the exit ramp is not "lose the customer" - it is "park them
intact." That makes it DEFERRED revenue, not lost revenue.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 5. HOW THE PREDICTION ACTUALLY WORKS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The technique is DISCRETE-TIME COMPETING RISKS SURVIVAL ANALYSIS. That name
sounds heavier than it is, so here is why each word is there.

  SURVIVAL ANALYSIS - the branch of statistics that models "how long until
  an event happens," and which handles the case where you stop watching
  before the event occurs.

  COMPETING RISKS - the case where several mutually exclusive things can
  happen, and whichever happens first prevents the others. Our three states
  are exactly that: a mandate can recover, or die, or be opted out of, but
  not two of them.

  DISCRETE-TIME - our clock ticks in attempt slots and billing cycles, not
  continuously. There are four slots, not a continuum.

THE KEY REASON THIS AND NOT A NORMAL CLASSIFIER:

Most mandates use up all four attempts without resolving. You never find out
what would have happened. A classifier trained on "did it recover, yes or
no" silently drops those cases or codes them as failures - either way it
biases every number toward the mandates that resolved quickly. Survival
analysis treats them correctly as RIGHT-CENSORED: we stopped watching, we
did not observe an outcome, and that is information rather than absence.

Getting this wrong is invisible. Everything still runs. The numbers just
quietly lie. That is why it is worth doing properly.

HOW IT IS IMPLEMENTED:

  1. Reshape the data into "person-period" format - one row per
     (mandate, attempt slot) rather than one row per mandate.
  2. Fit a multinomial logistic regression predicting, for each row, which
     of {recovered, dead, opted out, still pending} happens in that slot.
     This is mathematically a discrete cause-specific hazard model.
  3. Convert those hazards into cumulative incidence - the probability that
     each of the three outcomes has occurred by slot k.
  4. Calibrate with isotonic regression, so that when the model says 80%,
     it is right 80% of the time. Publish the reliability diagram.

Deliberately NOT used: deep survival models, transformers, DeepHit. They
exist for this task and they need data volume we do not have. Reaching for
them would also undermine the architectural argument below.

CHOOSING WHAT TO DO:

With four slots and three states, the decision space is small enough to
solve EXACTLY by backward induction - work backwards from the last slot,
computing the optimal action at each state. No reinforcement learning, no
heuristic search, no approximation. We solve it properly and say so.

The objective being maximised:

    expected money recovered
  - probability of opt-out  x  customer lifetime value
  - cost per attempt

  subject to:
    every attempt committed at least 24 hours in advance   (clause 6a)
    amount never above the mandate ceiling                 (clause 4c)
    amount above the AFA limit routed to re-authentication (clause 8a/8b)
    at most four attempts total                            (NPCI)
    stopping rules honoured

That middle term - the churn cost of a notification - is the contribution.
It is the term Stripe's model does not have because no equivalent regime
exists there, and the term Razorpay's fixed ladder ignores.

THE SIGNALS THE MODEL USES:

  Strong  : decline code granularity - "insufficient funds," "card expired,"
            and "mandate revoked" have completely different hazard shapes
  Strong  : an actual opt-out event, which is near-ground-truth exit intent
            and did not exist as an observable signal before April 2026
  Useful  : this customer's own history of successful debit timestamps,
            which reveals their replenishment rhythm without ever needing to
            see their balance
  Useful  : day of month, and the fact that on the 1st through 5th every SIP,
            EMI and insurance premium in the country competes for the same
            account balance - so retry timing is partly a CROWDING problem,
            not just a prediction problem
  Weak    : product engagement decline, if the merchant shares it


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 6. WHERE AI IS USED, AND WHERE IT DELIBERATELY IS NOT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This matters because the Buildathon scores "AI judgment - the right tool in
the right place, and where you chose not to use one."

NO LARGE LANGUAGE MODEL TOUCHES A MONEY DECISION.

The decision core is deterministic and statistical. This is not a
convention; it is enforced by a git hook that blocks any edit importing an
LLM client into the model, policy, or core directories. The guard script
ships in the repo as evidence.

Where models ARE used - language only:

  Claude Haiku : normalising decline strings, which differ wildly between
                 issuers and are unstandardised free text
  Claude Haiku : extracting cancellation intent from support ticket text,
                 including Hinglish
  Claude Sonnet: writing the merchant-facing root-cause narrative - once per
                 batch, never per transaction

All structured output goes through required tool-use, so malformed JSON is
structurally impossible rather than something caught downstream.

AND WE PROVE THE CHOICE RATHER THAN ASSERTING IT.

The repo contains a benchmark running an LLM-as-classifier against the
statistical model on the same held-out data, reporting four columns:

    accuracy (AUC) | p95 latency | cost per 1000 decisions | RUN-TO-RUN
                                                             VARIANCE

The variance column is the argument. Give the LLM the identical input five
times and it may return different retry times. In a payments path that is
disqualifying regardless of accuracy - because a decision you cannot
reproduce is a decision you cannot defend in a dispute.

The benchmark ships even though it goes against the LLM. Especially because
it does.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 7. THE SAFETY DESIGN
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There is an ugly asymmetry at the heart of this project.

If we wrongly decide a broke customer wants to leave, and offer them a
cancel button, WE CAUSED THE CHURN WE EXIST TO PREVENT. That error is far
more expensive than the reverse.

And exit intent is genuinely hard to detect from payment data alone. It is
what statisticians call weakly identified - the signal is there, but thin.

So the statistical limitation dictates the product's safety design:

  1. SPLIT CONFORMAL PREDICTION gates the off-ramp. Conformal prediction is
     a technique that gives a distribution-free coverage guarantee: calibrate
     on held-out data, and you can state the error rate as a mathematical
     property rather than an empirical hope. We only offer an exit when the
     conformal prediction set is the single value {WON'T PAY} at 95%
     coverage. Anything less certain stays in the retry lane.

  2. THE SYSTEM OFFERS; THE CUSTOMER DECIDES. It never cancels anything.
     This is enforced by the same guard that blocks LLM imports.

  3. BOTH ERROR COSTS ARE REPORTED - the cost of a missed recovery, and the
     cost of a false off-ramp. A single accuracy number hides the asymmetry
     that actually matters.

The connection between points 1 and 2 is the most defensible thing in the
whole submission: the model is not confident enough to act, so it proposes
and the human decides.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 8. HOW SUCCESS IS MEASURED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The evaluation is PRE-REGISTERED. On Day 1, before a single line of policy
code is written, the simulator configuration and the evaluation protocol are
committed to git and the commit hash is recorded in the repo. A hook then
blocks all further edits to those files.

This exists because the most common way an ML demo fools itself is tuning
the evaluation until the model wins. Making that mechanically impossible is
cheaper and more convincing than promising not to do it.

THE HEADLINE IS THREE BARS, NOT ONE:

    (a) money recovered
    (b) attempts spent
    (c) MANDATES PRESERVED     <- this is where we win

Compared against the incumbent baseline: the fixed T+1 / T+2 / T+3 ladder,
then halt. That is not a strawman we invented - it is Razorpay's documented
behaviour.

Every run happens twice, once under each compliance interpretation (strict
and permissive), because the regulation is ambiguous and we refuse to pick.

And every run happens across five stress regimes that were NOT used for
tuning: an issuer outage day, a delayed-salary week, a mandate-stacking
spike, festival season, and a competitor retry storm.

WE REPORT WHERE WE LOSE. A policy that wins in every regime is evidence the
regimes were tuned to it. An honest loss with an explanation scores higher
than a suppressed one.

Separately, a chaos harness kills the executor process 50 times at random
points mid-attempt, proving zero double-charges and zero lost jobs.

 9. WHY THIS FITS THE COMPETITION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Track 03 asks for an agent that detects revenue at risk, determines the
right intervention, and executes a bounded recovery workflow. Two of the
seven example directions it lists are literally this project: "mandate retry
sequencer" and "failed-subscription recovery."

The track's stated bar, and what answers it:

  "measured money recovered across a batch"
      -> 200-mandate batch, our policy against the fixed ladder,
         both numbers published

  "compliant escalation"
      -> amount-based routing at the Rs 15,000 authentication threshold;
         silent retry, then customer nudge, then re-authentication, each
         rung gated by what is actually permitted

  "stopping rules"
      -> attempt cap, opt-outs honoured permanently, revoked mandates never
         retried, and a stop when expected value turns negative

  "an audit trail"
      -> append-only ledger; every decision recorded with its reason, the
         rule that produced it, and its outcome

And the four judging criteria:

  Problem taste   -> involuntary churn is the largest silent leak in
                     subscription businesses, and the incumbent baseline is
                     documented rather than invented
  Build quality   -> integer paise throughout, append-only ledger,
                     idempotency proven under induced crashes, invariants
                     enforced by hooks rather than prose
  AI judgment     -> deterministic core with a measured benchmark explaining
                     why, not an assertion
  Failure recovery-> incidents logged live during the build, plus a chaos
                     harness and a deliberate failure shown in the video


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 10. WHAT THIS PROJECT CANNOT DO
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Stated plainly, because a submission that names its own limits is worth more
than one that pretends it has none.

  1. The evaluation is synthetic. It demonstrates that encoding real
     constraints beats ignoring them, under a stated model of the world
     calibrated against published statistics. It is NOT a lift number that
     transfers to production traffic, and it is not presented as one.

  2. Exit intent is weakly identified from payment data alone. Without
     merchant product-usage signals, intent accuracy is modest - which is
     precisely why the system offers rather than acts.

  3. Whether a retry requires its own fresh 24-hour notification is
     unresolved in the RBI circular. We ship both readings rather than
     guess. Lower-level NPCI and card-network operating rules may settle it;
     we have not seen those documents.

  4. Scope is deliberately narrow: failed recurring debits only. Not
     checkout abandonment, not one-time payment fraud, not receivables.

  5. Prior art exists. There is a granted patent on ML-optimised dunning
     using logistic regression or gradient boosting to score retry success.
     Stripe, Recurly and others ship commercial versions. We did NOT invent
     smart retries. The contribution is the three-way cause decomposition,
     the notification-cost term, and having "release the customer" in the
     action space at all.

 11. GLOSSARY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  MANDATE          Standing permission from a customer to their bank
                   allowing a merchant to debit them on a schedule.

  UPI AUTOPAY      India's recurring-payment mechanism over UPI.

  E-MANDATE        The general regulatory term for an electronic mandate,
                   across cards, UPI and prepaid instruments.

  INVOLUNTARY      Customers lost because a payment failed, not because
  CHURN            they chose to leave. 20-40% of total churn typically.

  DUNNING          The industry term for the process of chasing failed
                   payments.

  AFA              Additional Factor of Authentication - the second factor
                   (PIN, OTP) required above the transaction threshold.

  NPCI             National Payments Corporation of India - operates UPI
                   and NACH, and sets the retry attempt limits.

  RIGHT-CENSORED   A case where you stopped observing before the outcome
                   occurred. Handling these correctly is the main technical
                   reason for choosing survival analysis.

  HAZARD           The probability that an event occurs in a given time
                   interval, given it has not occurred yet.

  CUMULATIVE       The probability that a particular one of several
  INCIDENCE        competing outcomes has occurred by a given time.

  CONFORMAL        A method producing prediction sets with a guaranteed
  PREDICTION       error rate, without assuming a data distribution.

  CALIBRATION      Whether a model's stated confidence matches its actual
                   accuracy. A model saying 90% should be right 90% of
                   the time.

  IDEMPOTENCY      The property that performing an operation twice has the
                   same effect as performing it once. What prevents a
                   crashed-and-restarted process from double-charging.

  PRE-REGISTRATION Committing to your evaluation method before seeing
                   results, so you cannot tune the test to fit the answer.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 12. THE PITCH, IN ONE BREATH
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Every retry engine asks "will this payment succeed?"

  Ours asks "which of three things went wrong?" - because with four
  attempts and a cancel button attached to every notification, guessing
  wrong is expensive in both directions.

  And sometimes the right answer is to stop trying and hold the door.
