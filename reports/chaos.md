## Three things this report will not let you conclude

**The partition is over database statements, not over wall-clock time.** A
kill index is uniform over the observable side effects one attempt performs
(six database statements plus the provider call). That is a reproducible,
exhaustively coverable space; it is *not* a model of when a real process
dies. Real elapsed time is dominated by the network call, so a time-uniform
sampler would weight these windows very differently. The number below is the
denominator for *this* sampler and says nothing about production frequency.

**A kill signal cannot reach the most dangerous state at all.** Killing our
own process can only destroy work not yet done; it cannot make the provider
accept money and then lose the answer. That state is created on the far side
of the network, which is why the fault-seam block exists and is reported
separately. When the uniform block's unsafe-window count is low — and with
seed 0 at 24 kills it was zero — the correctness claim is carried entirely
by the fault-seam runs.

**A kill can only STOP a process, never DELAY one.** This harness is
single-process and single-threaded, so a *live but slow* worker is outside
its reachable state space entirely. That is not a small gap: the one real
double-charge found during this block (incident 5) lived exactly there, and
no amount of kill sampling would have surfaced it. Anything depending on
"nobody else is working on this key right now" is unproven by this report.

## What this block found

Three findings. Two were fixed; one caused a fix to be **withdrawn**.

1. **A permanently lost job** — `POSTMORTEM.md` incident 4, found on the
   harness's first execution. Recovery keyed its dangling scan on the lease
   table, which cannot see an `INTENT` row written before the lease was ever
   claimed. Fixed by scanning the ledger and using the lease only to exclude
   keys a live worker still holds. The regression test was proven
   discriminating against a verbatim reimplementation of the old algorithm.

2. **23 of 60 NPCI slots burned on provably-unsent attempts** — measured,
   fixed, and then **reverted**. See finding 3. The cost stands, reported
   above as `NPCI slots burned with NO SENT row`.

3. **A cross-generation double charge** — `POSTMORTEM.md` incident 5, found
   by the chaos-engineer review, *introduced by the fix for finding 2*. A
   worker stalled past its lease TTL is indistinguishable from a crashed one
   in durable state, so recovery voided a live worker's slot; the worker
   then completed its real charge and the freed slot was reissued at
   generation+1 — a different key — and charged again. Measured 2 charges
   with the fix, 1 without. Invisible to every oracle in this harness,
   because both oracles are keyed per receipt and the two charges land on
   two different keys. Resolved by reverting finding 2's fix rather than
   patching it: *a double-charge is worse than ten missed recoveries*, and
   the optimisation traded exactly that way.

The harness found findings 1 and 2 by interrupting the executor at points
nobody had written down. It could not have found finding 3, and did not —
that took a reviewer asking which states the harness *cannot construct*.
Both facts belong in the same report.
